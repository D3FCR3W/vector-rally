THE RULES OF THE ROAD

Click. Drive. Brake.

  1. One click takes your turn. Click the exact grid cell where you want to land. The 3×3 matrix is centered on your position plus your current velocity. It appears only when a human can drive. Green is clear; amber warns of a hazard; red means a crash or fatal damage. Hover to preview the path.
  2. Build speed, then brake. Each surrounding square adds −1, 0 or +1 to your velocity on each axis. Choose against your velocity to brake. The center, S or Space keeps your momentum. For example, moving right by 2 cells gives rightward landing choices 1, 2 or 3 cells away. Start in the direction of your car and the painted arrows, then follow the circuit clockwise. Every driver starts level on the same straight line.
  3. Watch the entire path. Track edges and cars cause a crash. Potholes damage tires and body without stopping or slowing you. Jumping over a hazard avoids it.
  4. Jump the gap. Drive straight along the yellow ramp's arrow with enough speed to jump the next cell and land beyond it. You can jump a hole or another car.
  5. Crashes limit speed. The car travels to the impact and stops on the last safe road cell. If every part survives, you can drive on your very next turn, with a speed limit of 1 grid cell for 3 turns (including diagonal steps). There are no skipped turns. Normal acceleration returns afterward unless oil or a puncture restricts it. AI follows the same rules.
  6. A living world. Pick Forest, Desert, Futuristic, Jungle, Sky, Sea or Space before racing. Each world has its own scenery, crash effects and hidden-route trails. Birds and probes land and take off; creatures and rovers cross the road. Help and the menu pause them; reduced-motion mode keeps them still.
  7. Finish one full lap. Pass three checkpoints and cross the finish line clockwise. Backing over the line does not win.
  8. Watch for wildlife. Ending a move on a grounded animal, landed drone or rover causes a crash. Passing its cell is safe. Flying animals are clear of the road. An animal walking onto a parked car never triggers a crash. The landing warning updates as wildlife moves.
  9. Oil and police spikes. A hard crash spills oil. Crossing it forces straight-line momentum and spins your car for your next three turns; only center is available. Police deploy spikes from round 2: crossing them damages tires and allows only braking for three turns. At rest, center spends a turn. Oil takes priority if both effects overlap.
  10. Keep your car alive. Tires, engine and body have separate condition bars. Losing any part means retirement. Harder impacts cause more damage. The cyan PIT detour is only one cell wide: pass through its white service cross for full repair and removal of every penalty, without stopping. Reach it before a part hits zero.
  11. Frame your race. AI drivers move in one smooth group until the next human turn. Follow stays with a human. Frame offers Nearby rivals (the racer ahead and behind), All drivers, or Choose drivers. Dragging or zooming frees the camera; Follow or Frame resumes tracking.
  12. Explore the mountains. Some shortcuts run under rock. Look for dark entrances: the roof fades to show the tunnel interior when a viewed car enters and returns when it leaves. Other worn paths remain open. The same road and collision rules apply underground.
